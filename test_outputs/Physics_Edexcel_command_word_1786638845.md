**Evaluate — GCSE Physics (Edexcel)**

**1. Official Definition & Examiner Expectation**
• **Evaluate** means: **judge the value, importance, or effectiveness of something using evidence and reasoning**.  
• In GCSE Physics, this usually means you must:
  • **weigh up the pros and cons**  
  • **use relevant physics facts/data**  
  • **reach a justified conclusion**  
• Examiners expect:
  • more than a description  
  • more than one point of view if possible  
  • a clear final judgement based on evidence

**2. How to Structure Full-Mark Answers**
• A strong answer usually follows this pattern:
  • **State the point**
  • **Explain why it matters using physics**
  • **Compare it with another factor**
  • **Finish with a justified conclusion**
• Useful phrases:
  • “This is effective because…”
  • “However…”
  • “A limitation is…”
  • “Compared with…”
  • “Therefore, overall…”
• For a full-mark evaluation, aim to include:
  • **relevant physics terminology**
  • **comparison**
  • **supporting data or evidence** if given
  • **a final decision/judgement**

**3. Common Student Mistakes & Pitfalls**
• **Only describing** instead of judging  
  • e.g. listing facts without deciding which is better  
• **Giving one-sided answers**  
  • not considering any disadvantages or alternative factors  
• **No conclusion**
  • answers end without a final overall judgement  
• **Unsupported opinions**
  • saying “it is good” without explaining why  
• **Ignoring the context/data in the question**
  • not using the numbers, graph, or scenario provided  
• **Confusing evaluate with explain**
  • “explain” tells why; “evaluate” tells why **and how well/how suitable**

**4. Example Demonstration**
• **Incorrect:** “Solar panels are good because they use sunlight and do not make pollution.”  
• **Correct:** “Solar panels are effective because they produce electricity without greenhouse gas emissions, but they are intermittent and depend on sunlight. Overall, they are a good long-term energy source if storage is available.”
