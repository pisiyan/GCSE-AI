# 3) Topic 1 — Key concepts in biology

## Cell types and structures

### Animal cells
Sub-cellular structures:
- **nucleus**: contains DNA; controls the cell
- **cell membrane**: controls movement of substances in and out
- **mitochondria**: site of aerobic respiration
- **ribosomes**: site of protein synthesis

### Plant cells
Animal cell structures plus:
- **cell wall**: supports the cell, made of cellulose
- **chloroplasts**: site of photosynthesis, contain chlorophyll
- **vacuole**: contains cell sap, maintains turgor

### Bacterial cells
Prokaryotic cells have:
- **chromosomal DNA**: circular DNA in cytoplasm
- **plasmids**: small rings of DNA
- **cell membrane**
- **ribosomes**
- **flagellum/flagella**: movement

---

## Specialised cells
Cells are adapted to their function.

### Sperm cell
Adaptations:
- **tail** for swimming
- **many mitochondria** for energy
- **acrosome** with enzymes to digest egg membrane
- **haploid nucleus** to combine with egg nucleus

### Egg cell
Adaptations:
- **nutrients in cytoplasm** for early embryo
- **haploid nucleus**
- **cell membrane changes after fertilisation** to stop more sperm entering

### Ciliated epithelial cells
Adaptations:
- **cilia** move mucus and trapped particles
- line airways, oviducts, etc.

---

## Microscopy
### Light microscopes
- can view living cells
- lower resolution and magnification than electron microscopes

### Electron microscopes
- much higher resolution and magnification
- allow ultrastructure to be seen
- cannot view living specimens

### Why this matters
Improved microscopy has helped scientists discover:
- mitochondria
- ribosomes
- membranes and organelles in more detail

---

## Size and scale
You need to understand:
- **millimetre (mm) = 10⁻³ m**
- **micrometre (µm) = 10⁻⁶ m**
- **nanometre (nm) = 10⁻⁹ m**
- **picometre (pm) = 10⁻¹² m**

### Common conversions
- 1 mm = 1000 µm
- 1 µm = 1000 nm

---

## Enzymes

### What enzymes are
- biological catalysts
- speed up chemical reactions
- not used up in reactions

### Active site
- enzymes have a specific **active site**
- substrate fits exactly into active site

### Lock-and-key idea
- substrate and enzyme are complementary
- explains specificity

### Denaturation
- high temperature or extreme pH changes enzyme shape
- active site changes
- substrate no longer fits
- enzyme stops working

---

## Factors affecting enzyme activity
### Temperature
- as temperature increases, reaction rate increases up to optimum
- above optimum, enzyme denatures and rate drops quickly

### pH
- each enzyme has an optimum pH
- too acidic or alkaline changes shape of active site

### Substrate concentration
- more substrate = more frequent collisions
- rate increases until all active sites are full
- then rate levels off

---

## Rate calculations
You may be asked to calculate rate:
- **rate = change ÷ time**
- e.g. rate of starch breakdown or product formation

---

## Biological molecules
### Carbohydrates
- sugars and starch
- main energy source

### Proteins
- made of amino acids
- used in growth, enzymes, hormones, antibodies

### Lipids
- fats and oils
- energy store, insulation, cell membranes

### Food tests
- **starch**: iodine → blue-black
- **reducing sugar**: Benedict’s + heat → green/yellow/orange/brick-red
- **protein**: Biuret test → lilac/purple
- **lipids**: emulsion test → milky white

---

## Transport in cells

### Diffusion
Movement of particles:
- from high concentration to low concentration
- down a concentration gradient
- passive, no energy needed

### Osmosis
- diffusion of water through a partially permeable membrane
- from dilute solution to concentrated solution

### Active transport
- movement against concentration gradient
- requires energy from respiration

---

## Osmosis in potatoes
In the potato practical:
- potato in different sucrose solutions
- measure initial and final mass
- calculate percentage change

### Percentage change in mass
\[
\text{percentage change}=\frac{\text{change in mass}}{\text{original mass}} \times 100
\]

# 4) Topic 2 — Cells and control

## Mitosis
### Cell cycle stages
- **Interphase**: cell grows, DNA replicates
- **Prophase**: chromosomes condense
- **Metaphase**: chromosomes line up in middle
- **Anaphase**: chromatids pulled apart
- **Telophase**: nuclei reform
- **Cytokinesis**: cell splits into two

### Why mitosis is important
- growth
- repair of damaged tissue
- asexual reproduction

### Result of mitosis
- two genetically identical daughter cells
- same number of chromosomes as parent cell
- body cells are **diploid**

---

## Cancer
- caused by uncontrolled cell division
- may form tumours
- can spread to other parts of the body

---

## Growth and differentiation
### Animals
- growth by cell division and cell enlargement
- cells differentiate early in development

### Plants
- growth continues throughout life
- occurs in **meristems**
- due to cell division, elongation, and differentiation

### Differentiation
- cells become specialised
- important because different cells do different jobs

---

## Stem cells
### Embryonic stem cells
- can become many different cell types
- used in research and medicine

### Adult stem cells
- found in some tissues
- less versatile than embryonic stem cells

### Plant meristems
- areas of active cell division
- can produce new plant cells

### Pros and cons of stem cells
**Benefits**
- replace damaged tissues
- treat disease
- medical research

**Risks/ethical issues**
- may be rejected
- tumour formation
- ethical concerns with embryos

---

## Nervous system
### Main parts
- **sensory receptors** detect stimulus
- **sensory neurone** carries impulse to CNS
- **relay neurone** connects neurones in CNS
- **motor neurone** carries impulse to effector
- **synapse** gap between neurones

### Neurone structure
- **dendron/dendrite**: receives impulse
- **axon**: carries impulse away
- **myelin sheath**: insulates, speeds transmission

### Synapse
- neurotransmitter released
- diffuses across gap
- binds to receptors on next neurone

---

## Reflex arc
A fast automatic response:
1. stimulus detected
2. impulse travels along sensory neurone
3. relay neurone in CNS
4. motor neurone sends impulse
5. effector acts

---

## Eye and vision
Key structures:
- **cornea** and **lens** focus light
- **iris** controls pupil size
- **retina** contains rods and cones

### Rod cells
- work in dim light
- black and white vision

### Cone cells
- colour vision
- work in bright light

### Eye defects
- **short-sightedness**: image forms in front of retina
- **long-sightedness**: image forms behind retina
- **cataracts**: lens becomes cloudy
- **colour blindness**: faulty cone cells

### Corrections
- short-sightedness: concave lens
- long-sightedness: convex lens
- cataracts: surgery

# 5) Topic 3 — Genetics

## Reproduction

### Asexual reproduction
Advantages:
- one parent needed
- rapid
- no mate needed

Disadvantages:
- no variation
- vulnerable to disease change

### Sexual reproduction
Advantages:
- variation in offspring
- more chance some survive environmental change

Disadvantages:
- needs mate
- slower

---

## Meiosis
- produces **gametes**
- four daughter cells
- each has **half** the number of chromosomes
- cells are genetically different

### Importance
- maintains chromosome number after fertilisation
- creates variation

---

## DNA structure
DNA is a polymer made of:
- two strands forming a **double helix**
- strands joined by **complementary base pairs**
- weak **hydrogen bonds**
- **nucleotides** = sugar + phosphate + base

Bases:
- A pairs with T
- C pairs with G

---

## Genome and genes
- **genome**: entire DNA of an organism
- **gene**: section of DNA coding for a protein

---

## Protein synthesis
### Transcription
- RNA polymerase binds to DNA
- complementary mRNA is made
- mRNA leaves nucleus

### Translation
- mRNA attaches to ribosome
- codons read in triplets
- tRNA brings amino acids
- amino acids linked into polypeptide

### Why this matters
The base sequence of a gene determines:
- amino acid sequence
- protein shape
- protein function

---

## Inheritance terms
Know these precisely:
- **chromosome**: long DNA molecule carrying genes
- **gene**: section of DNA coding for a protein
- **allele**: version of a gene
- **dominant**: expressed if present
- **recessive**: expressed only if two copies present
- **homozygous**: two same alleles
- **heterozygous**: two different alleles
- **genotype**: allele combination
- **phenotype**: observable characteristic
- **gamete**: sex cell
- **zygote**: fertilised egg

---

## Monohybrid inheritance
Use Punnett squares and genetic diagrams to predict outcomes.

### Probability
- dominant and recessive inheritance can be expressed as:
  - ratios
  - percentages
  - probabilities

### Sex determination
- eggs always carry X chromosome
- sperm carry X or Y
- XX = female
- XY = male

---

## Variation
### Genetic variation
Caused by:
- mutation
- sexual reproduction

### Environmental variation
Caused by:
- diet
- climate
- lifestyle
- upbringing

### Mutations
- most have no effect
- some small effect
- rarely major effect

---

## Human Genome Project
This produced a map of human DNA.

### Uses
- identifying genes for disease
- improving diagnosis
- personalised medicine

### Limitations
- ethical concerns
- lots of unknown function in DNA
- expensive and time-consuming

---

# 6) Topic 4 — Natural selection and genetic modification

## Natural selection
### Darwin and Wallace
They developed the theory that species change over time through selection of advantageous inherited features.

### Darwin’s theory
1. individuals in a species vary
2. more offspring are produced than survive
3. competition occurs
4. best-adapted survive and reproduce
5. advantageous alleles become more common

---

## Antibiotic resistance
- random mutations may make bacteria resistant
- antibiotics kill non-resistant bacteria
- resistant bacteria survive and reproduce
- population becomes resistant

This is evidence for evolution by natural selection.

---

## Human evolution evidence
### Fossils
Examples:
- Ardi
- Lucy
- fossils discovered by Richard Leakey

### Stone tools
- show changes over time
- can be dated by surrounding rocks and materials

### Pentadactyl limb
Same basic bone pattern in many animals:
- evidence of common ancestry
- supports evolution

---

## Classification
Genetic analysis led to **three domains**:
- Bacteria
- Archaea
- Eukaryota

This replaced the simpler five kingdom system.

---

## Selective breeding
Humans choose parents with desired characteristics.

### Advantages
- improved yield
- disease resistance
- useful traits in animals/plants

### Disadvantages
- reduced variation
- inherited defects
- often ethical concerns

---

## Tissue culture
- plant cells grown in sterile conditions
- used to make clones
- useful in medicine and plant breeding

---

## Genetic engineering
Artificially changing an organism’s genome to give a desired characteristic.

### Main stages
- isolate gene
- cut DNA with **restriction enzyme**
- create **sticky ends**
- insert gene into **vector** (often plasmid)
- join with **ligase**
- transfer into host organism

### GM crops
Example: inserting Bt gene for insect resistance.

### Benefits
- increased yield
- less pesticide
- improved resistance

### Risks
- gene transfer to wild species
- reduced biodiversity
- ethical concerns

---

# 7) Topic 5 — Health, disease and development of medicines

## Health
WHO definition:
- physical, mental and social well-being
- not just absence of disease

---

## Disease types
### Communicable
- caused by pathogens
- spread between organisms

### Non-communicable
- not caused by pathogens
- often linked to lifestyle, genetics, environment

---

## Pathogens
Disease-causing organisms:
- viruses
- bacteria
- fungi
- protists

---

## Examples of diseases
- **cholera**: bacteria; causes diarrhoea
- **TB**: bacteria; lung damage
- **chalara ash dieback**: fungus; leaf loss/bark lesions
- **malaria**: protist; damages blood and liver
- **HIV**: virus; destroys white blood cells
- **Ebola**: virus; haemorrhagic fever
- **Helicobacter**: bacterium; stomach ulcers

---

## Transmission and prevention
### Examples
- cholera: contaminated water
- TB: airborne droplets
- chalara: airborne spores
- malaria: mosquito vector
- HIV: body fluids / sexual contact / blood
- Helicobacter: oral transmission

### Prevention
- hygiene
- vaccination
- isolation
- vector control
- safe sex
- clean water

---

## The immune system
### Physical and chemical barriers
- skin
- mucus
- cilia
- lysozymes
- hydrochloric acid

### Specific immune response
1. pathogen enters body
2. antigens trigger response
3. antibodies are produced
4. memory lymphocytes remain
5. second infection gives faster stronger response

---

## Immunisation
- uses inactive/pathogenic material
- stimulates immune response
- creates memory cells without severe disease

### Herd immunity
If enough people are immunised, disease spread decreases.

---

## Antibiotics
- only work against bacteria
- bacteria have cell processes different from host cells
- antibiotics do not kill viruses

---

## Aseptic technique
Used to avoid contamination in culturing microbes:
- sterilise equipment
- use autoclave
- use sterile loops
- keep lids on
- work near Bunsen burner

---

## Antibiotic testing practical
You may investigate:
- antiseptics
- antibiotics
- plant extracts

Measure:
- clear zones of inhibition

---

## Developing new medicines
Stages:
1. discovery
2. development
3. preclinical testing
4. clinical testing

---

## Monoclonal antibodies
### Production
- lymphocytes produce desired antibodies
- fused with tumour cells to make **hybridoma**
- hybridoma divides and makes antibodies

### Uses
- pregnancy tests
- cancer diagnosis and treatment
- locating blood clots

### Advantages
- specific
- targets certain cells

### Disadvantages
- expensive
- can cause side effects
- ethical concerns with use of animals

---

## Non-communicable diseases
Examples:
- cardiovascular disease
- cancer
- lung disease
- liver disease
- malnutrition-related disease

### Risk factors
- smoking
- alcohol
- poor diet
- lack of exercise
- obesity

### BMI formula
\[
BMI = \frac{mass\,(kg)}{height\,(m)^2}
\]

---

## Cardiovascular disease treatments
- lifelong medication
- surgery
- lifestyle changes

Evaluate by considering:
- effectiveness
- cost
- side effects
- prevention versus treatment

# 8) Topic 6 — Plant structures and their functions

## Photosynthesis
Photosynthetic organisms are the **main producers** of biomass.

### Word equation
carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

### Conditions
- light
- chlorophyll

### Why it is endothermic
- absorbs light energy

---

## Limiting factors
### Temperature
- enzyme-controlled reactions
- too low = slow
- too high = enzymes denature

### Light intensity
- more light = faster photosynthesis until another factor limits

### Carbon dioxide concentration
- more CO2 increases rate until saturation

---

## Interactions of limiting factors
One factor can limit even if others are optimal.

---

## Light intensity and inverse square law
- light intensity decreases with distance
- proportional to \(1/d^2\)

So doubling distance reduces light intensity to a quarter.

---

## Root hair cells
Adapted to absorb water and mineral ions:
- large surface area
- thin cell wall
- many mitochondria for active transport
- long extension into soil

---

## Xylem and phloem
### Xylem
- dead, lignified cells
- transport water and mineral ions
- provide support

### Phloem
- living cells
- transport sucrose
- uses energy
- translocation can be both directions

---

## Transpiration
Loss of water vapour from leaves.

### How it happens
- water evaporates from mesophyll
- diffuses out through stomata
- creates transpiration pull

### Stomata
- pores controlled by guard cells
- allow gas exchange

---

## Leaf structure
A leaf is adapted for:
- photosynthesis
- gas exchange

Features:
- large surface area
- thin
- chloroplasts near top
- air spaces
- stomata

---

## Water uptake
Affected by:
- light intensity
- air movement
- temperature

---

## Transpiration rate calculations
Use rate = amount ÷ time.

---

## Plant hormones
### Auxin
Controls:
- phototropism
- gravitropism

### Commercial uses
- rooting powders
- weedkillers
- gibberellins in germination and fruit growth
- ethene in ripening

---

# 9) Topic 7 — Animal coordination, control and homeostasis

## Hormones
Hormones are chemical messengers produced by endocrine glands and carried in blood.

### Glands include
- pituitary
- thyroid
- pancreas
- adrenal glands
- ovaries
- testes

---

## Adrenaline
Produced in fight-or-flight response:
- increases heart rate
- increases blood pressure
- increases blood to muscles
- raises blood glucose

---

## Thyroxine
Controls metabolic rate.

### Negative feedback
- low thyroxine → TRH from hypothalamus
- TRH stimulates TSH from pituitary
- TSH stimulates thyroid
- when thyroxine returns to normal, TRH and TSH decrease

---

## Menstrual cycle
Hormones involved:
- FSH
- LH
- oestrogen
- progesterone

### Key roles
- FSH: egg maturation
- LH: ovulation
- oestrogen: repairs uterus lining
- progesterone: maintains uterus lining

### If no fertilisation
- progesterone falls
- lining breaks down
- menstruation occurs

---

## Contraception
### Hormonal methods
- prevent ovulation
- thicken cervical mucus
- change uterine lining

### Barrier methods
- prevent sperm reaching egg
- condoms protect against STIs

### ART
Examples:
- IVF
- clomifene therapy

---

## Homeostasis
Keeping internal conditions stable.

### Why important
- enzymes work best in a narrow range
- cells can function properly

---

## Thermoregulation
### Skin
- epidermis: outer layer
- dermis: contains blood vessels, sweat glands, receptors

### Responses to heat
- vasodilation
- sweating
- hairs lie flat

### Responses to cold
- vasoconstriction
- shivering
- hairs stand up

---

## Blood glucose control
### Insulin
- released by pancreas
- lowers blood glucose
- promotes glucose uptake and glycogen formation

### Glucagon
- released by pancreas
- raises blood glucose
- causes glycogen to glucose in liver

### Diabetes
#### Type 1
- pancreas doesn’t produce insulin
- treated with insulin injections and monitoring

#### Type 2
- body cells don’t respond properly
- linked to obesity and inactivity
- treated by diet, exercise, medication

---

## Urinary system and kidneys
### Structure
- kidneys
- ureters
- bladder
- urethra

### Nephron function
- filtration in glomerulus and Bowman’s capsule
- selective reabsorption of glucose
- reabsorption of water

### ADH
- controls permeability of collecting duct
- increases water reabsorption when dehydrated

### Kidney failure
Treatments:
- dialysis
- transplant

### Urea
Produced in liver from excess amino acids.

---

# 10) Topic 8 — Exchange and transport in animals

## Need for transport
Organisms need:
- oxygen
- carbon dioxide
- water
- nutrients
- mineral ions
- urea

---

## Exchange surfaces
Need to be adapted for fast diffusion:
- large surface area
- thin membrane
- good blood supply
- ventilation

### Surface area : volume ratio
As organisms get bigger:
- SA:V decreases
- transport becomes more difficult
- specialised exchange systems are needed

---

## Alveoli
Adapted for gas exchange:
- large surface area
- moist lining
- thin walls
- rich capillary network

Oxygen diffuses into blood, carbon dioxide diffuses out.

---

## Diffusion factors
Rate increases with:
- larger surface area
- greater concentration gradient
- shorter diffusion distance

### Fick’s law
Rate of diffusion is proportional to:
\[
\frac{\text{surface area} \times \text{concentration difference}}{\text{thickness of membrane}}
\]

---

## Blood
### Red blood cells
- contain haemoglobin
- carry oxygen
- biconcave shape

### White blood cells
- phagocytes engulf pathogens
- lymphocytes produce antibodies

### Plasma
- transports dissolved substances

### Platelets
- help blood clot

---

## Blood vessels
### Arteries
- thick muscular walls
- carry blood away from heart
- high pressure

### Veins
- thinner walls
- valves prevent backflow

### Capillaries
- one-cell-thick walls
- exchange materials with tissues

---

## Heart and circulation
- right side pumps deoxygenated blood to lungs
- left side pumps oxygenated blood to body
- valves prevent backflow
- left ventricle has thicker wall because it pumps to whole body

---

## Respiration
### Definition
Exothermic reaction releasing energy in living cells.

### Aerobic respiration
glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy

### Anaerobic respiration in animals
glucose → lactic acid + energy

### Anaerobic respiration in yeast/plants
glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide + energy

---

## Respiration practical
Often uses a respirometer to measure oxygen uptake.

---

## Cardiac output
\[
cardiac\ output = stroke\ volume \times heart\ rate
\]

---

## Interdependence

## Sampling

Used to estimate abundance of plants or slow-moving organisms.

### Belt transect

### Why sampling matters

- count organisms in sample area

### Trophic levels
- producer
- primary consumer
- secondary consumer

### Energy transfer
Energy is lost as:
- heat
- movement
- waste
- uneaten material

---

## Biomass pyramids
Usually shaped like a pyramid because biomass decreases at each trophic level.

---

## Human impacts on ecosystems
### Fish farming
Can increase food production but may pollute water and spread disease.

### Non-indigenous species
Can outcompete native species.

### Eutrophication
Caused by fertiliser runoff:
- algae bloom
- light blocked
- plants die
- decomposers use oxygen
- fish die

---

## Biodiversity
Benefits:
- stable ecosystems
- food security
- medicines
- ecosystem resilience

### Reforestation
- restores habitats
- increases carbon uptake
- supports biodiversity

---

## Food security
Affected by:
- population growth
- meat consumption
- pests/pathogens
- climate change
- sustainability of agriculture

---

## Material cycles
### Carbon cycle
Processes:
- photosynthesis
- respiration
- decomposition
- combustion

### Water cycle
Processes:
- evaporation
- transpiration
- condensation
- precipitation

### Nitrogen cycle
- nitrates needed for proteins and DNA
- made available by bacteria
- fertilisers and crop rotation help maintain soil nitrogen

---

## Decomposition
Affected by:
- temperature
- water
- oxygen

### Composting
Warm, moist, oxygen-rich conditions increase decay.

# 12) High-priority equations to memorise

- **BMI** = mass ÷ height²
- **Cardiac output** = stroke volume × heart rate
- **Surface area : volume ratio** = surface area ÷ volume
- **Fick’s law**:
  \[
  \text{rate} \propto \frac{\text{surface area} \times \text{concentration difference}}{\text{thickness}}
  \]
