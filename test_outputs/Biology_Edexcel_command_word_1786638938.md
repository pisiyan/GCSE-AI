**Evaluate — Official Definition & Examiner Expectation**

• **Evaluate** means you must **judge how good or reliable something is** by using evidence, strengths, weaknesses, and sometimes limitations.  
• In Edexcel GCSE Biology, this usually means you should **weigh up both sides** and then **make a justified conclusion**.  
• Examiners expect you to go beyond describing results: you must **comment on quality, reliability, validity, or usefulness**.  
• A strong evaluation often includes:
  • **Positive points**  
  • **Negative points / limitations**  
  • **A final judgement** based on the evidence

---

**How to Structure Full-Mark Answers**

• **1. State what is being evaluated**
  • Briefly identify the experiment, method, data, or claim.

• **2. Give a strength**
  • Use phrases like:
    • “A strength of this method is…”
    • “This supports the conclusion because…”

• **3. Give a limitation**
  • Use phrases like:
    • “However, a weakness is…”
    • “This may reduce reliability/validity because…”

• **4. Support points with evidence**
  • Refer to actual data, method details, sample size, repeats, variables, or anomalies.

• **5. Finish with a judgement**
  • Use phrases like:
    • “Overall, this is reliable because…”
    • “Therefore, the conclusion is only partly valid because…”
    • “On balance, the evidence suggests…”

**Helpful exam phrase bank**
• “This makes the results more reliable because…”  
• “This reduces validity because…”  
• “A larger sample size would improve…”  
• “Repeating the investigation would help to…”  
• “There is not enough evidence to conclude…”  

---

**Common Student Mistakes & Pitfalls**

• **Only describing** what happened, rather than judging it.  
• **Not giving both sides** — only strengths or only weaknesses.  
• **No conclusion** at the end.  
• **Vague comments** like “it was good” or “it was bad” without explaining why.  
• **Not linking to biology** — comments should relate to reliability, validity, accuracy, or evidence.  
• **Ignoring data** in the question when it should be used in the evaluation.  
• **Mixing up command words**:
  • *Describe* = what happens  
  • *Explain* = why it happens  
  • *Evaluate* = judge how good it is and justify

---

**Example Demonstration**

• **Incorrect:** “The experiment was good because the results were interesting.”  
• **Correct:** “The experiment is fairly reliable because it was repeated, but the small sample size reduces confidence in the conclusion.”
