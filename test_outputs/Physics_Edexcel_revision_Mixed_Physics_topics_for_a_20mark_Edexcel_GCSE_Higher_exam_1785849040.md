# Mixed Physics Topics for a 20-Mark Edexcel GCSE Higher Exam  
## Revision Notes and Exam-Answer Toolkit

A 20-mark mixed-topic question is usually **synoptic**: it links ideas from different parts of the course, often including:
- **calculations**
- **explanations using key physics terms**
- **comparison/evaluation**
- **practical-method / data-analysis points**
- **why a method or design is better**

Higher-tier questions often reward:
- using **equations correctly**
- linking **cause → effect → conclusion**
- using **specialist vocabulary**
- writing **well-structured paragraphs**
- giving **scientific reasons**, not just statements

---

# 1) What Edexcel expects in a high-mark Physics answer

## A strong 20-mark response usually:
1. **States the physics clearly**
2. **Uses correct equations and units**
3. **Explains why something happens**
4. **Links multiple topics together**
5. **Evaluates or justifies a choice**
6. **Draws a conclusion supported by evidence**

### Common command words
- **Describe** = say what happens
- **Explain** = give the reason why
- **Compare** = give similarities and differences
- **Evaluate** = discuss strengths/weaknesses and make a judgement
- **Justify** = give reasons why one option is better
- **Calculate** = show working and include units

---

# 2) Core exam strategy for a 20-mark mixed question

## Use this structure:
### Point
Give the physics statement.

### Because
Explain the reason using scientific ideas.

### Therefore
Link it to the scenario or conclusion.

Example structure:
- The current increases because resistance decreases.
- This is because the circuit has a lower total resistance.
- Therefore the component transfers energy faster and becomes hotter/brighter.

# 3) High-value mixed-topic knowledge to revise

---

## A) Motion, forces and momentum

### Key equations
- **speed = distance ÷ time**
- **acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time**
- **force = mass × acceleration**
- **weight = mass × gravitational field strength**
- **momentum = mass × velocity**
- **force = change in momentum ÷ time**

### Must-know ideas
- **Scalar** = size only, e.g. speed, distance, mass, energy
- **Vector** = size and direction, e.g. velocity, force, acceleration, momentum
- **Resultant force = 0** means:
  - object is stationary, or
  - moving at constant velocity
- **Newton’s 2nd law**:
  - bigger force → bigger acceleration
  - bigger mass → smaller acceleration for same force
- **Momentum conservation**:
  - total momentum before collision = total momentum after collision
- **Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance**
- Factors affecting stopping distance:
  - speed
  - reaction time
  - mass of vehicle
  - road conditions
  - brake condition
  - friction

### Good explanation phrases
- “The resultant force is zero, so the velocity stays constant.”
- “Increasing mass increases inertia, so the acceleration decreases.”
- “A larger change in momentum in the same time gives a bigger force.”

---

## B) Energy, work, power and efficiency

### Key equations
- **work done = force × distance**
- **power = work done ÷ time**
- **kinetic energy = ½mv²**
- **gravitational potential energy = mgh**
- **efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input**

### Important ideas
- Energy is **conserved**: it cannot be created or destroyed.
- In real systems, energy is often **dissipated** into the surroundings.
- **Dissipated energy** is usually transferred as heat, making it less useful.
- Mechanical systems become wasteful when energy is lost by:
  - friction
  - sound
  - heating
- Improve efficiency by:
  - lubrication
  - insulation
  - reducing resistance
  - streamlining

### Common exam links
- A falling object transfers **GPE → KE**
- A moving car losing speed transfers **KE → thermal energy**
- A motor uses electrical energy and transfers it to **kinetic energy** and heat
- A kettle transfers electrical energy mostly to **thermal energy** of the water

### Good explanation phrases
- “The energy is not destroyed; it is transferred to the surroundings.”
- “A device with greater efficiency wastes less energy.”
- “Increasing power means energy is transferred more quickly.”

---

## C) Electricity and circuits

### Key equations
- **charge = current × time**
- **potential difference = current × resistance**
- **energy transferred = charge × potential difference**
- **energy transferred = current × potential difference × time**
- **power = current × potential difference**
- **power = current² × resistance**

### Circuit rules
- **Current** is the rate of flow of charge.
- **Ammeter** in series.
- **Voltmeter** in parallel.
- In a **series circuit**:
  - current is the same everywhere
  - potential difference is shared
  - total resistance increases
- In a **parallel circuit**:
  - potential difference is the same across each branch
  - current splits
  - total resistance decreases

### Component behaviour
- **Fixed resistor**: straight-line I–V graph
- **Filament lamp**: resistance increases as temperature rises
- **Diode**: current flows mainly in one direction only
- **LDR**: resistance decreases as light intensity increases
- **Thermistor (NTC)**: resistance decreases as temperature increases

### Domestic electricity
- UK mains supply:
  - **a.c.**
  - **50 Hz**
  - about **230 V**
- Safety features:
  - **earth wire**
  - **fuse**
  - **circuit breaker**
- Switches and fuses should be in the **live wire**

### Good explanation phrases
- “A larger resistance reduces the current for the same p.d.”
- “Electrical energy is transferred as thermal energy due to collisions between electrons and ions.”
- “A fuse melts if the current becomes too large, breaking the circuit.”

---

## D) Waves, light and the electromagnetic spectrum

### Key equations
- **wave speed = frequency × wavelength**
- **wave speed = distance ÷ time**

### Wave facts
- Waves transfer **energy and information**, not matter.
- **Transverse waves**: vibration perpendicular to direction of travel
- **Longitudinal waves**: vibration parallel to direction of travel
- Sound is longitudinal; EM waves are transverse

### Refraction
- Occurs when a wave changes speed entering a new medium
- Towards normal = slows down
- Away from normal = speeds up

### EM spectrum order
**radio → microwaves → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays**

### Higher-tier important ideas
- Higher frequency = higher energy and greater danger
- All EM waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum
- EM waves can be reflected, absorbed, transmitted or refracted differently depending on wavelength

### Uses and dangers
- **Microwaves**: cooking, satellite communications
- **Infrared**: heaters, remote controls, thermal imaging
- **Visible light**: seeing, photography
- **UV**: security marking, fluorescent lamps, sterilising
- **X-rays**: medical imaging
- **Gamma**: sterilisation, cancer treatment

### Good explanation phrases
- “The wave changes direction because its speed changes.”
- “Higher frequency radiation has more energy, so it is more dangerous.”
- “A lens refracts light to form real or virtual images.”

## E) Thermal physics and particle model

### Key ideas
- Matter is made of tiny particles.
- **Solid**: particles vibrate in fixed positions
- **Liquid**: particles close together but can move past each other
- **Gas**: particles far apart and moving quickly

### Density
- **density = mass ÷ volume**

### Internal energy
- Total energy stored in particles
- Heating increases internal energy
- Temperature rise = particles move faster
- Change of state = energy used to overcome forces between particles

### Equations
- **change in thermal energy = mcΔθ**
- **thermal energy for change of state = mL**

### Gas pressure
- Pressure is due to particles colliding with container walls
- Higher temperature → particles move faster → more frequent/harder collisions → higher pressure
- Smaller volume → collisions more frequent → higher pressure

### Good explanation phrases
- “During melting, energy is used to overcome intermolecular forces, not raise temperature.”
- “A gas can be compressed because there is lots of empty space between particles.”
- “Higher density means more mass in the same volume.”

---

## F) Magnetism, electromagnetism and induction

### Key ideas
- Like poles repel, unlike poles attract
- Magnetic fields go from **north to south**
- Field strength is shown by how close the field lines are
- A current in a wire creates a magnetic field
- A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field experiences a force
- This is the **motor effect**

### Key equation
- **force on conductor = magnetic flux density × current × length**

### Induction
- A changing magnetic field induces a potential difference
- Moving a magnet relative to a conductor can generate current
- In generators:
  - **alternator** produces a.c.
  - **dynamo** produces d.c.

### Transformers
- Only work with **a.c.**
- **step-up** transformer increases voltage
- **step-down** transformer decreases voltage
- National grid uses high voltage because:
  - current is smaller
  - heating losses are reduced
  - transmission is more efficient

### Good explanation phrases
- “A greater current produces a stronger magnetic field.”
- “A changing magnetic field induces a potential difference.”
- “High-voltage transmission reduces current, so less energy is wasted as heat.”

---

## G) Atomic structure and radioactivity

### Atom structure
- Nucleus contains:
  - protons
  - neutrons
- Electrons move around nucleus in shells/orbits
- Most mass is in the nucleus
- Atom is mostly empty space

### Radiation types
- **Alpha**: helium nucleus
- **Beta minus**: electron emitted from nucleus
- **Beta plus**: positron emitted
- **Gamma**: electromagnetic radiation

### Properties
- **Alpha**: strongly ionising, weakly penetrating
- **Beta**: moderately ionising and penetrating
- **Gamma**: weakly ionising, strongly penetrating

### Half-life
- Time taken for activity/count rate to fall by half
- Decay is random; you cannot predict when one nucleus decays
- But half-life lets you predict the behaviour of a large sample

### Uses and dangers
- Smoke alarms
- Sterilising equipment
- Food irradiation
- Tracers
- Cancer treatment

### Good explanation phrases
- “Alpha is stopped by paper because it ionises strongly and loses energy quickly.”
- “Beta is more penetrating than alpha but less than gamma.”
- “Radioactive decay is random, but half-life is predictable for a large number of nuclei.”

# 4) Common calculation types to practise

## 1. Multi-step force/motion calculations
Example chain:
- find acceleration
- use **F = ma**
- use **work done = force × distance**
- use **KE = ½mv²**

## 2. Energy transfer calculations
- electrical energy: **E = IVt**
- thermal energy: **Q = mcΔθ**
- change of state: **Q = mL**

## 3. Circuit calculations
- **V = IR**
- **P = VI**
- **E = QV**
- **Q = It**

## 4. Waves
- **v = fλ**
- rearrange carefully and include units

## 5. Density and pressure
- **ρ = m/V**
- **P = F/A**
- **P = hρg** for liquids

# 5) How to answer a 20-mark mixed question well

## A. Use linked paragraphs
If the question covers, for example, a car safety device:
- mention **momentum**
- mention **force and time**
- mention **stopping distance**
- mention **energy transfer**
- possibly mention **materials or crumple zones**

## B. Include both equations and explanations
Do not just calculate. Explain what the numbers mean.

## C. Use comparative language
- greater than
- less than
- increases
- decreases
- therefore
- because
- as a result

## D. Finish with a conclusion
If the question asks for a judgement, clearly state:
- which option is best
- why it is best
- any limitation

# 6) Exam phrases that gain marks

- “This shows that…”
- “This is because…”
- “As a result…”
- “Therefore…”
- “The evidence suggests…”
- “The most efficient option is…”
- “The resultant force is zero, so…”
- “Energy is conserved, but…”
- “The current increases because resistance decreases…”
- “This causes a greater rate of energy transfer…”

---

# 7) Common mistakes to avoid

- Confusing **mass** and **weight**
- Writing **speed** when the question asks for **velocity**
- Forgetting units
- Using the wrong equation
- Saying energy is “lost” instead of **dissipated**
- Saying current is “used up”
- Forgetting that **current is the same in series**
- Mixing up **alpha, beta and gamma**
- Explaining without giving reasons
- Not answering the actual command word
