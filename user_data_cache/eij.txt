

## SECTION 1: Islam: Beliefs

### Question 01.1

* **Question:** Which one of the following terms means that God is involved in the world?
* **Answer:** **B Immanent**

---

### Question 01.2

* **Question:** Give two Muslim beliefs about angels.
* **Answer:** 1. Muslims believe that angels are spiritual beings created by Allah from light, meaning they do not possess physical bodies, free will, or biological needs like eating or sleeping.
2. Angels have specific duties assigned to them by God; for example, Jibril (Gabriel) brings God's revelation to the prophets, and Izrail is the Angel of Death responsible for taking souls.

---

### Question 01.3

* **Question:** Explain two ways in which the revelation of the Qur'an influences Muslims today.
* **Answer:** 1. **Source of moral and legal guidance:** The Qur'an acts as the ultimate authority for the Shariah (Islamic law), influencing how Muslims live their daily lives, including what they eat, how they handle finances, and how they interact with others.
2. **Spiritual connection and worship:** Reciting or listening to the Qur'an in Arabic deepens a believer's relationship with Allah. It is used in daily prayers (Salah) and provides comfort, guidance, and peace during difficult times.

---

### Question 01.4

* **Question:** Explain two Muslim beliefs about the Day of Judgement. Refer to sacred writings or another source of Muslim belief and teaching in your answer.
* **Answer:** 1. **Accountability and resurrection:** Muslims believe that on the Day of Judgement (Yawm al-Qiyamah), all human beings will be resurrected and held fully accountable by Allah for their actions, intentions, and deeds done during their earthly lives.
2. **Heaven (Jannah) or Hell (Jahannam):** Based on God's final judgement, individuals will be sent either to paradise (Jannah) as a reward for a righteous life or to hell (Jahannam) as a consequence of unrepented evil. This is supported by the Qur'an, which states: *"And every small and great thing is recorded"* (Surah 54:53), emphasizing that nothing goes unnoticed.

---

### Question 01.5

* **Question:** 'Tawhid (the Oneness of God) is the most important Muslim belief.' Evaluate this statement.
* **Answer:** * **Arguments supporting the statement:** * Tawhid is the foundational pillar of Islam, forming the first part of the Shahadah ("There is no god but Allah").
* It asserts that God is unique, supreme, and without partners, rejecting any form of shirk (polytheism or idolatry), which is considered the ultimate sin in Islam.
* Every other article of faith—such as belief in angels, prophets, and predestination—flows directly from the absolute oneness and sovereignty of God.
* **Arguments supporting a different point of view:** * Some might argue that belief in the final prophets (such as Muhammad) or the final accountability on the Day of Judgement is equally crucial because humans can only know how to worship the One God through the specific messengers and scriptures provided.
* Others may argue that practical pillars, like giving Zakah or establishing Salah, are the ultimate test of faith since belief without action is incomplete.


* **Conclusion:** * While practical duties and prophetic history are vital components of the religion, Tawhid remains the absolute cornerstone. Without the Oneness of God, the entire structure of Islamic theology collapses, making it indisputably the most important belief.

## SECTION 2: Islam: Practices

### Question 02.1

* **Question:** Which one of the following is the name for Friday prayers in Islam?
* **Answer:** **D Jummah**

---

### Question 02.2

* **Question:** Give two ways in which a Muslim prepares for Salah (prayer).
* **Answer:** 1. Performing **Wudu** (ritual washing), which involves washing the hands, mouth, nose, face, arms, head, and feet with clean water to achieve ritual purity.
2. Ensuring they are wearing clean, modest clothing and facing the direction of the **Qibla** (the Kaaba in Makkah).

---

### Question 02.3

* **Question:** Explain two contrasting ways in which Muslims celebrate Id-ul-Adha.
* **Answer:** 1. **Performing Qurbani (animal sacrifice):** Many Muslims in communities worldwide or during Hajj participate in sacrificing an animal (like a sheep, goat, or cow) to commemorate Prophet Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, with the meat divided among family, friends, and the poor.
2. **Community prayers and festive gatherings:** Muslims start the day by attending special morning prayers (Salah) at the mosque or an open-air ground (Eidgah), followed by visiting family, exchanging gifts, and enjoying large festive meals together.

---

### Question 02.4

* **Question:** Explain two reasons why Muslims give Zakah. Refer to sacred writings or another source of Muslim belief and teaching in your answer.
* **Answer:** 1. **Purification of wealth and soul:** Giving a fixed percentage (2.5%) of one's accumulated wealth annually helps purify the giver from greed, selfishness, and materialism, teaching them self-discipline.
2. **Supporting the needy and building community welfare:** Zakah acts as a mandatory welfare system to bridge the gap between rich and poor, ensuring that society's most vulnerable are cared for. The Qur'an frequently links Zakah with regular prayer, emphasizing its importance, such as in the verse: *"And establish prayer and give zakah"* (Surah 2:43).

---

### Question 02.5

* **Question:** 'Following Muslim practices is difficult in the modern world.' Evaluate this statement.
* **Answer:** * **Arguments supporting the statement:** * Fasting during Ramadan can be extremely challenging for Muslims living in non-Muslim majority countries where school or work schedules do not accommodate long summer fasts or lack flexible break times.
* Finding permissible (Halal) food or taking time out of a busy workday to perform the five daily prayers (Salah) at their precise times can clash with modern corporate environments.
* Social pressures, such as avoiding alcohol or interest-based banking (Riba), can isolate young Muslims from modern peer groups.
* **Arguments supporting a different point of view:** * Islam is designed to be a practical way of life; Allah does not burden any soul beyond what it can bear.
* Modern workplaces and schools are increasingly accommodating, offering multi-faith prayer rooms and flexible arrangements.
* Technology has also made practices easier, with smartphone apps providing accurate prayer times, Qibla directions, and easy access to digital Qur'ans and Halal food locators.


* **Conclusion:** * While contemporary lifestyle demands and secular environments present distinct logistical hurdles, the core practices remain entirely manageable and rewarding for committed Muslims, supported by legal accommodations and modern technology.
